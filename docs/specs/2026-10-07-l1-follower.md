# One L1 follower, read once per finality step

Status: steps 1 to 3 built; steps 4 and 5 in progress.

## 1. Problem

Three services read the L1 on their own, and each polls on its own clock:

| Service | Reads | Cadence on staging |
|---|---|---|
| da-watcher | the finalized tip, then per block its header and the lockbox logs | a tip poll every 1 s |
| l1-indexer | the finalized tip, the settlement logs of a range, then per block its header and the lockbox logs | a tick every 12 s |
| batcher | the age of its last post (`BatchPosted` logs and the block time) | every 10 s, plus its own transactions |

This has three costs:

- **RPC cost.** On Alchemy (20 compute units for a block read, 60 for a log query) the staging followers use about 125M units a month, about $65 at pay-as-you-go prices. The free plan has 30M.
- **Three views of L1.** Each service has its own source set, its own cross-check and its own chain check. A lying source halts them at different blocks, and the operator has three cursors to reason about. On 2026-10-01 the indexer and the da-watcher halted at different blocks on the same lie.
- **Wasted polls.** Finality on Ethereum and Sepolia moves 32 blocks at a time, about every 6.4 minutes. A poll between two steps finds nothing.

The l1-indexer already reads each finalized block once, takes the settlement and lockbox logs, and derives each block's epoch with the same `derive_epoch` the da-watcher uses. The other two services repeat its work.

## 2. Design

### 2.1 The follower

The l1-indexer becomes the L1 follower: the only service that reads L1 data. The crate and the job keep their names.

Each tick:

1. Read the finalized tip (one call).
2. If the tip moved, the range is the blocks after the cursor up to the tip: normally one finality step of 32 blocks.
3. Read the logs of the settlement and the lockbox for the range in one query with both addresses. A provider caps the span of one query (Alchemy's free plan: 10 blocks; pay-as-you-go: no cap), so the follower splits the range into chunks of `--max-log-range` blocks (default 10).
4. Read the header of every block in the range, in one JSON-RPC batch request. The chain records an epoch for every L1 block and checks that each block descends from the previous one, so every hash is needed. The batch saves round trips, not compute units.
5. Check the chain: each header's parent hash equals the previous header's hash, and the first equals the cursor's hash. Where a light client is configured, the last header's hash must equal the light client's finalized header for that number; the parent chain then verifies every header of the step.
6. Derive each block's epoch, archive the range (batches, payloads, epochs, as today), publish the range on the stream (2.2), then write the cursor.

**The poll follows the finality schedule.** The finalized tip moves once per finality step: 32 slots of 12 s, about every 6.4 minutes, on the epoch boundaries of the beacon chain. The follower computes the next expected step from the genesis time and the slot length (as `BeaconApi::slot_of` does today), sleeps until it, then reads the tip every 12 s (`--poll-interval-secs`, one slot) only until the tip moves, and sleeps again. A late step (missed slots, a non-finalizing chain) keeps the 12 s reads going until it comes. So:

- a step reaches the consumers within about 12 s of finality, and a deposit waits for L1 finality (about 13 minutes) plus at most one slot;
- the tip reads cost two or three per step instead of thirty: about 0.5M units a month per source.

Three rules keep the sleep from hiding anything:

- **Halts retry every slot.** While the follower is halted (an unreachable source, a chain break, a disagreement), it reads every slot instead of sleeping to the next step, so a halt clears within one slot of its cause.
- **Readiness follows the plan.** A rule of "the last read is fresher than two poll periods" would fail between steps. The follower exports `kardamom_l1_follower_next_wake_seconds` (the planned wake time) and is ready while now is before that time plus one slot; an alert fires when a wake is overdue.
- **No schedule, fixed interval.** A chain with no beacon genesis time or slot schedule (anvil in dev and in the chaos shards, with its fast finality) polls at the fixed `--poll-interval-secs`. The aligned poll is on only when the follower has a beacon API to read the genesis time from, so the chaos cases keep their deposit timing.

The poll interval does not drive the da-watcher's re-publish or the sequencer's `origin_gap` (section 3): the re-publish fires on unconfirmed epochs, which a slow stream does not create, and records arrive in order, so a slow poll creates no origin gap. The interval only adds deposit latency, and the aligned poll keeps that to one slot.

**Sources.** The follower reads through the cross-checked source set of #481: a block's hash is accepted when two sources agree, and a disagreement is a halt. The light client anchor of step 5 verifies the headers. It does not verify the logs, since a source can omit a log without breaking the hash chain, so logs keep the two-source rule.

**Where the checks live.** Today each follower runs the two-source cross-check and the chain check on its own reads. With this design they run once, in the follower, before a block enters `l1_blocks`: a record on the stream is a block that two sources agreed on, that descends from the previous record, and whose step ends at the light client's finalized header. The consumers check its parent link against their own cursor and compare the complete payload when two instances publish the same block. The independent check downstream stays where it is: the validator's origin rules (#509) and its own L1 content check (2.5). No check is lost; the duplicated ones go.

### 2.2 The `l1_blocks` stream

A new Aeron stream `l1_blocks`, id 1020, recorded by the archive like `tx_deposits`:

```
L1Block {
    number:      u64,
    hash:        B256,
    parent_hash: B256,
    timestamp:   u64,
    epoch:       Epoch,            // the derive_epoch output: origin and deposits
    batches:     Vec<BatchEntry>,  // the BatchPosted events of this block
}
```

One record per finalized L1 block, in block order. A consumer resumes from the archive at a block number and checks the hash of the record before it against its own cursor. The stream carries finalized blocks only, so a record never changes.

### 2.3 Two follower instances

The follower runs with `count = 2` on two nodes. Both read and both publish. A consumer takes the first record for each block number and drops a second only when its complete canonical record agrees, including the epoch, deposits and batches. The header hash alone does not commit the RPC log response: two records can carry the same header hash but different derived payloads. A different header or payload for one block is a halt of the consumer with cause `l1_follower_disagreement`: one of the instances has a lying source the cross-check did not catch. One instance down costs nothing. Both down pause every consumer through the `events` stream, with the follower as the root.

### 2.4 The consumers

| Consumer | Today | After |
|---|---|---|
| da-watcher | reads L1, derives epochs, publishes them on `tx_deposits` | reads `l1_blocks`, publishes each record's epoch on `tx_deposits`. No L1 access. Its cursor keeps its meaning (section 3). |
| batcher | polls for its last post's age; reads `lastBatchIndex` and `l2BlockEnd` at start | learns its posts and their L1 time from the `batches` of each record; computes `kardamom_batcher_last_post_age_seconds` from them. It keeps direct RPC for its own writes only: nonce, fee, `eth_sendRawTransaction`, the receipt of its own transaction, and the one contract read at start. |
| validator | checks epochs through the light client | unchanged: the verifier must not read through the producer's path (2.5). |
| rebuild tool | reads the indexer's archive | unchanged. |

The da-watcher could fold into the follower as a task. It stays a separate process in this design: a fault in the follower then halts the reader, not the publisher of the chain's epochs, and the change stays small. Folding is a later choice.

### 2.5 The verifier stays independent

The validator checks the epochs that the da-watcher puts on the chain. If the validator read the same `l1_blocks` stream, a lie that passed the follower would pass the validator too. So the validator keeps its own L1 read, at the same finality-step cadence:

- the last header of each step against the light client's finalized header, and the parent chain back through the epochs on the chain;
- the lockbox logs of the step from a source that is not the follower's first source.

That is one more reader of about 6M units a month (2.6), and it is the price of an independent check.

### 2.6 Cost

Per finality step of 32 blocks on Alchemy's free plan: one tip read (20), four log queries (4 x 60, the 10-block cap), 32 headers (32 x 20), so 900 units. At about 225 steps a day, that is about 6M units a month. Tip reads cost 20 each, two or three per step with the aligned poll (2.1): about 0.5M a month.

| Reader | Units a month |
|---|---|
| each follower instance, per source (two instances x two sources) | about 7M each; about 28M total |
| validator's independent read | about 7M |
| batcher's own transactions | under 1M |
| **Total, today's pattern for comparison: about 125M** | **about 36M across two providers** |

With both follower instances reading both providers, each provider sees about 14M follower units plus its share of the independent validator and batcher reads (about 18M if evenly split), inside the assumed free plan's 30M. These estimates use the unit prices above and must be checked against actual provider usage; batching does not share a read between follower instances. A mainnet chain costs the same: the block rate is the same.

### 2.7 Halts and pauses

Every halt uses the halt record and a runbook (spec 2026-10-03, section 3.6):

| Where | Cause | Clears |
|---|---|---|
| follower | `l1_source_disagreement`, `l1_chain_break`, `l1_unreachable` (exists) | Auto when the cause clears |
| follower | `l1_light_client_mismatch`: the step's last header is not the light client's finalized header | Operator |
| consumer | `l1_follower_disagreement` (2.3) | Operator |

A consumer whose follower is halted, or whose stream has no record for longer than three finality steps, is Paused with the follower as its root. It resumes by itself.

An alert `l1_follower_lag` fires when the newest record on `l1_blocks` is more than two finality steps (about 13 minutes) behind the finalized tip that the validator's independent read sees. It names the follower and its runbook. It fires before any downstream symptom of a stalled follower, such as the batcher's post-age alert, so the page points at the cause.

## 3. The da-watcher's cursor stays as built

The da-watcher has a durable cursor since #511 and #513: `L1Cursor { number, hash }` in the file of `--l1-cursor-file`, written through `CursorFile<V>`, with the halt `l1_cursor_unreadable`. It holds the sealer's confirmed L1 origin C (the `l1_origin` of the boundary egress) and that block's hash, not the last block it published. This design keeps all of that. What changes is only where the da-watcher reads L1 data:

| Behavior (#513) | Today | After |
|---|---|---|
| Start | waits up to 20 s for the first boundary, resumes after the sealer's origin S, and reads the hash of S through the cross-checked sources | the same, with the hash of S from the `l1_blocks` record of S (its `hash` field). It replays the stream from S + 1. |
| A sealer fleet rebuild moves the origin back | the watcher follows S backwards | the same: the stream's archive is read from the new S + 1. If S is older than the stream archive holds, the da-watcher reads the epochs from the follower's archive (the indexer's epoch store, which keeps every epoch since its start block). |
| Window C + 1 .. P of published, unconfirmed epochs (at most 2048), re-published after 30 s without progress of C | kept in memory | kept in memory as now. After a restart the window is refilled from the stream's archive from C + 1, not from RPC. |
| Parent check of the next block against the cursor's hash | through the RPC header | through the record's `parent_hash` |
| The loop | `DaWatcher::step` selects on the poll tick, the re-publish deadline (`republish_at`) and `confirmation.changed()` | the stream's next record replaces the poll tick as one arm of the same `select!`. The re-publish and confirmation arms stay, so the cursor follows the sealer's commit and a re-publish fires on time while no record arrives. |
| `--l1-resume-after` | wins at start only | unchanged |
| The stream has no record of S at start (the follower is down, or its archive does not hold S yet) | not applicable | the da-watcher waits. It logs once a minute, sets `kardamom_da_watcher_waiting_for_l1_block{number}`, and is Paused on the events stream with the follower as root. It never publishes without the parent check, and it has no RPC fallback: it has no L1 access. |

So the stream must keep at least the blocks from the oldest origin a sealer can restore to. A sealer fleet rebuild seeds from the posted head, so its origin moves back to M, the L1 origin of the batcher's posted head, which can be far behind the live origin. The floor of the `l1_blocks` archive is therefore M, the same floor as spec 2026-10-03 section 3.10: nothing at or above the L1 origin of the posted head is purged. The follower's epoch store is the backstop below M, for an operator's `--l1-resume-after`. It serves each epoch in the same bytes the stream carries: the `derive_epoch` output with its deposits and their canonical ids, so the sealer's dedup sees the same epoch from either source. A test checks that an epoch read from the store and from the stream is byte-equal.

## 4. Chaos cases

The `chaos-l1` shard's L1 fault proxy sits in front of the follower's sources. Its cases change their target from three readers to one:

| Case | Injection | Assertion |
|---|---|---|
| `l1-liar` (changed) | the existing faults through the proxy | the follower halts once; the da-watcher and the batcher pause with the follower as root; all resume when the fault clears |
| `follower-instance-loss` (new) | stop one follower instance during load | the consumers see no gap; epochs and the batcher's post age continue |
| `follower-total-loss` (new) | stop both follower instances for three finality steps, then start them | the consumers pause and resume; the da-watcher resumes after the sealer's origin with no gap and no double epoch |
| `follower-disagreement` (new) | point one instance's sources at the proxy with `WrongBlockHash` past the cross-check (both of its sources lie the same way) | the consumers halt with `l1_follower_disagreement`; the validator's independent check also fails the epoch |

## 6. Decided

- The follower is the l1-indexer. It already reads each block once and derives each epoch.
- The poll follows the finality schedule: it sleeps until the next expected step and reads every slot only until the tip moves. A fixed interval either wastes reads between steps or adds deposit latency.
- The validator keeps its own read. A verifier that reads through the producer's path verifies nothing.
- The da-watcher stays a separate process for now.

## 7. Open questions

1. The light client anchor needs the light client's finalized header by number. Helios serves its finalized checkpoint and its last 256 heads; at a 32-block step, the finalized header of each step is inside that window.
2. `--max-log-range` could be learned from the provider's error answer instead of being set. A setting is simpler and explicit; learning it is a follow-up if providers differ in practice.

## 8. Decisions made in the build

The build filled these gaps. Each one is a choice within section 2.

- **The light client anchor applies at the tip.** The follower checks the last header of a range
  against the light client only when the range ends at the finalized tip. Helios serves its last 256
  heads and its finalized checkpoint, so a range in the middle of a catch-up is outside its window. The
  two-source rule covers those ranges, and the anchor of the range that reaches the tip covers them
  through the parent chain. A light client that does not answer at the tip is `l1_unreachable`.
- **The schedule comes from the beacon API.** `--beacon-api` gives the genesis time,
  `SECONDS_PER_SLOT` and `SLOTS_PER_EPOCH` (`/eth/v1/beacon/genesis`, `/eth/v1/config/spec`). The
  deploy passes the light client's consensus endpoint. The next step is the next epoch boundary.
- **A catch-up reads at once.** A range that ends below the finalized tip reads the next range without
  a sleep, with or without a schedule.
- **Readiness has 10 s of slack.** The follower is ready while now is before its planned wake plus one
  slot plus 10 s, for the round trips of a tick. The wake alert fires 2 minutes after a missed wake.
  An operator halt plans a wake every slot while it waits, so only the halt alert pages.
- **The lag alert reads the follower's own tip until step 5.** `l1_follower_lag` compares the newest
  record with the follower's finalized tip. Step 5 replaces the tip with the validator's independent
  read.
- **The follower publishes nothing before its recording is live.** `--archive-durability` needs
  `[discovery]`: the recorder of each follower node records every follower publication, so each
  archive is a full mirror.
- **Each follower node records two topics at most.** The node meta `archive_topics_follower` is
  `l1_blocks` on a node with the `indexer` role, beside `archive_topics`. The local profile runs the
  second instance on `ingress-0`.
- **Nothing purges the recording.** No retention purges an Aeron recording today, so the floor M of
  section 3 holds by construction. A future purge keeps every block at or above M.
- **The archive keeps each record.** `blocks/<n>.rkyv` holds the `L1Block` record; the API serves it as
  `indexer_l1_block`. It is the backstop below the stream archive.
- **The instance count is a deploy value.** `L1_INDEXER_COUNT` (default 2) lets a deployment with one
  `indexer` node run one instance.
- **The consumer rule is a pure check and a take.** `L1BlockDedup::admit` decides, and the consumer
  calls `take` only after it handled the record, so a record it could not publish stays the next one.
- **The da-watcher's history has two sources.** A history read replays every recording of `l1_blocks`
  from the archives of the follower nodes (a record names its block, so no archive position is needed)
  and keeps the records from the wanted block on. Where the replay stops short or does not reach the
  wanted block, the follower's archive serves the rest by number (`indexer_l1_block`). The e2e stacks
  run without an archive and read the follower's archive alone.
- **A housekeeping tick stays in the loop.** The stream's next record replaces the poll tick as an arm
  of `select!`. A housekeeping tick (`--poll-interval-secs`, 1 s in the deploy) does what no record
  starts: a history read the watcher waits on, a publish to retry, the waiting log, the check of the
  follower, and the readiness mark. It reads no L1.
- **The silence root.** A paused consumer names the follower as its root. When every live follower
  instance is halted, the root is that halt, from the `events` board, which the da-watcher now reads.
  When the stream is silent (`--l1-silence-secs`, default 1152 s: three finality steps) or the watcher
  waits for a record for longer than one tick, no instance names a halt: the root is
  `l1-indexer/l1_blocks` with the cause `l1_unreachable`.
- **A chain break on the consumer clears by itself.** A record that does not name the head as its
  parent is `l1_chain_break`: the watcher drops it and reads the block again from the archives every
  tick. A second hash for one number is `l1_follower_disagreement`, cleared by an operator; the watcher
  takes no record until the clear.
- **Every deployment runs the follower.** The da-watcher reads its stream, so the deploy runs the
  follower with the in-cluster anvil too, every second (anvil has no beacon API).
- **The chaos follower reads two sources.** In the `chaos-l1` shard the follower reads the fault proxy
  and the proxy's second source (`/second`), which serves L1 except a fault scoped to the caller. So a
  lie is a disagreement: the follower halts, publishes none of it, and resumes by itself. With one
  source, a wrong hash at the end of a range reached the stream and the chain, and the stream's archive
  then held two records of that block; the cases no longer need an operator step.
