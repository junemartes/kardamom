# The transaction canary: a user's view of the chain, all the time

Status: steps 1, 2 and 2b built. Step 3 (staging) is an operator step.

## 1. Problem

Every signal the operator has today is an internal one: a service's metrics, its halt
record, its alerts. On 2026-10-01 every L2 signal stayed green for two days while the
chain's data stopped reaching L1, because nothing looked at the chain the way a user does:
send a transaction, see it confirmed, see it reach L1, move funds in from L1.

The canary is a small service that does exactly that, without pause, and reports each
success and each failure as a metric. It is the end-to-end check: when it fails and no
internal alert fires, the internal signals have a blind spot.

## 2. What it checks

The canary runs probes. Each probe runs on its own timer and reports
`kardamom_canary_probe_total{probe, outcome}`, a latency histogram per stage, and
`kardamom_canary_last_success_timestamp_seconds{probe}`.

| Probe | Cadence | What it does | Stages timed | Failure outcomes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `transfer` | every 5 s | an EIP-1559 transfer between the canary's L2 accounts, through each ingress endpoint in turn (not only the load balancer) | submit to hash, hash to `offered`, `sealed`, `executed` on the status feed, hash to receipt | `rpc_error{code}` (for example `-32010` DA lag, fee errors), `rejected{reason}` from the status feed, `receipt_status_0`, `timeout{stage}`, `nonce_gap` |
| `contract` | every 60 s | a call that writes a counter in a small canary contract, then a read that must return the new value (see section 9: the read is `eth_getBalance` of the counter, which holds one wei per write) | submit to receipt, receipt to read | the above, plus `state_mismatch` |
| `fees` | every 5 min | checks a receipt against the fee rules of spec 2026-10-01-priority-fees: the base fee is the header's, `priorityFeePaid` equals the tip rate times the gas limit, `eth_feeHistory` and `eth_maxPriorityFeePerGas` answer | none | `fee_mismatch{field}` |
| `safe` | each transfer, sampled 1 in 60 | waits until the transaction's block is at or below the `safe` tag (the batcher's posted head) | receipt to safe | `timeout{stage="safe"}` after twice the batcher's idle flush |
| `deposit` | every 6 h | deposits a small amount on L1 through the lockbox from the canary's L1 account, and waits for the L2 credit | L1 inclusion, L1 finality, L2 credit | `l1_error`, `timeout{stage}` |
| `rwa` | every 2 min | mints the test RWA token to an allowlisted ring account, transfers it between ring accounts, and tries one transfer to an address off the allowlist, which must revert | submit to receipt | the above, plus `supply_mismatch` (total supply is not the sum of mints less burns), `allowlist_breach` (the off-list transfer succeeded) |
| `swap` | every 60 s | swaps L2 ETH for the RWA token in the canary pool, then the reverse on the next run; checks the amount out against the constant-product formula from the reserves before the swap, that the product of the reserves does not fall, and that the reserves equal the pool's balances | submit to receipt | the above, plus `swap_mismatch`, `invariant_broken`, `reserve_mismatch` |
| `liquidity` | every 6 h | adds a small amount of liquidity, then removes it on the next run; the returned shares and amounts must match the pool's formula | submit to receipt | the above, plus `liquidity_mismatch` |
| `read` | every 15 s | `eth_blockNumber` on each ingress endpoint must advance; a receipt of an old transaction must still answer (the persisted receipt path) | none | `head_stalled{endpoint}`, `receipt_lost` |

The status stages come from the notifier's feed (`kardamom_subscribeTxStatus` with the
sender filter, spec 2026-10-01-transaction-status-events). A missing stage that the later
stages imply is not a failure (the spec says a client treats a missing earlier stage as
implied); a missing `executed` past the timeout is only a failure when no receipt or later authoritative result proves execution. Track feed delivery gaps separately from transaction failures.

## 3. The test RWA token and the pool

Two small contracts under `contracts/src/canary/`, deployed by the canary at its first start (section 5: deploy if the stored address is empty), with Foundry tests next to the other contracts:

- **`CanaryRwa`**: an ERC-20 test token named "Kardamom Canary Asset", symbol `KCA`, 18 decimals. It is made up and stands for no real asset; it exists only on the chain the canary runs on. The canary's owner account mints and burns; transfers succeed only between addresses on an allowlist the owner keeps, as a real-world-asset token does. The `rwa` probe exercises the mint, the allowlist and the supply.
- **`CanaryPool`**: a constant-product pool for L2 ETH and `CanaryRwa`, written for this purpose (about a hundred lines: `addLiquidity`, `removeLiquidity`, `swapEthForToken`, `swapTokenForEth`, `getAmountOut` with a 0.3% fee, reserves synced to balances). It is our own code: copying Uniswap would pull GPL-licensed code into the repository for a test contract. The pool is on the allowlist.

At first start the canary mints 1,000,000 test tokens to its owner account, adds 0.002 ETH and 100,000 tokens as the pool's first liquidity, and allowlists its ring. Each swap moves 0.00001 ETH or the token amount the pool quotes for it, so the pool stays balanced over the alternating runs.

## 4. Accounts and funds

- **L2 accounts:** a small ring (default 4) from one mnemonic, so one stuck nonce does not
  stop the probe. One owner per account allocates nonces for all probe tasks and persists
  each in-flight transaction's nonce, hash and signed bytes before submission. A timeout
  or unknown error does not prove rejection: reconcile the receipt and committed/pending
  nonce, and retry the same signed transaction before assigning that nonce to different
  work. Restart restores this journal. An unresolved transaction blocks that account,
  while another ring account can continue; report the stalled nonce separately.
- **L1 account:** one key for the `deposit` probe. It is funded once by the operator, and it
  funds the L2 ring through its first deposit. On staging the first funds come from the
  batcher's balance: a one-time L1 transfer of 0.01 ETH from the batcher to the canary's L1
  account (an operator action at setup, not code in the canary; the canary never holds the
  batcher's key).
- **Gauges:** `kardamom_canary_balance_wei{layer, account}`. An info alert fires when the L2
  ring or the L1 account falls under a floor; the canary then stops the probes that would
  fail for lack of funds and reports `outcome="unfunded"` instead of noise.
- **Pool funds:** the first liquidity needs 0.002 L2 ETH, so the first deposit is 0.005 ETH.
- **Spend on staging:** each L2 transfer pays 21,000 gas at the L2 base fee plus a zero tip,
  to the chain's beneficiary; each deposit is one L1 transaction (about 0.0001 ETH at recent
  Sepolia prices), four a day.

The keys live in the seat folder and reach the task through Nomad Variables, like every
other secret of the deploy.

## 5. The service

- A new crate `crates/canary`, binary `kardamom-canary`, built on the `kardamom-load` library
  (`crates/bench`: signers, mnemonic derivation, presigned transfers, the call workflow) and
  `kardamom_obs` (the exporter, readiness, `host_id`).
- A Nomad job `canary.nomad.hcl`, `count = 1`, on the aux node, with the ingress endpoints from
  Consul (each instance, not the load balancer, so a single bad ingress shows), the notifier's
  WebSocket, the L1 RPC for the `deposit` probe, and the lockbox and settlement addresses.
- It deploys its contracts (the `contract` probe's counter, `CanaryRwa`, `CanaryPool`) at start if the addresses it stores are empty; the bytecode comes from the Foundry build of `contracts/`, and the addresses go to its data directory.
- The probes have independent deadlines; a probe that fails does not stop unrelated work.
  Counter writes and reads have one owner. Pool swap and liquidity probes serialize their
  reserve snapshot, transaction and verification, so another canary probe cannot change
  reserves between measurement and assertion. RWA supply checks use the same ownership
  rule for mints and burns. Shared signers use the per-account nonce owner from section 4.
  Distinguish intervening external contract activity from an EVM invariant failure; a
  moving snapshot alone must not page as a chain fault.
- The canary is an observer: it does not use the halt record and does not publish on the
  `events` stream. Its failures are metrics.

## 6. Grafana and alerts

A dashboard `kardamom-canary.json`: the success ratio per probe, the pool's reserves and price, the RWA token's supply, the stage latencies (p50,
p95, p99), the failures by outcome, the last success per probe, the time to `safe`, the
deposit times, and the balances.

Alerts (`deploy/alerts.yml`, routed to Telegram by severity):

| Alert | Rule | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| `KardamomCanaryFailing` | the success ratio of `transfer` or `contract` under 95% over 10 min | page |
| `KardamomCanaryStalled` | no success of `transfer` for 5 min | page |
| `KardamomCanaryNotSafe` | a sampled transaction not at `safe` within twice the idle flush | page |
| `KardamomCanaryDepositLate` | no L2 credit 30 min after the deposit's L1 finality | page |
| `KardamomCanaryFeeMismatch` | any `fee_mismatch` | page |
| `KardamomCanaryStateFault` | any `supply_mismatch`, `allowlist_breach`, `swap_mismatch`, `invariant_broken`, `reserve_mismatch` or `liquidity_mismatch`: the EVM computed a wrong result | page |
| `KardamomCanaryLowFunds` | a balance under its floor | info |

An Alertmanager inhibit rule mutes the canary's pages while a root halt pages: one incident,
one page, with the halt's runbook. So a canary page means users fail while every internal
signal says the chain is fine, which is the blind spot of 2026-10-01.

## 7. Proof

- Unit tests per probe against the e2e harness's local stack. Include an accepted submit
  whose response is lost, restart with an in-flight nonce, concurrent probe requests for
  one signer, and delayed status events after a receipt. Concurrent swap/liquidity work
  must not produce a false invariant alarm.
- A chaos case in the `chaos-l1` shard: freeze the batcher past the DA-lag budget; the
  canary's `transfer` probe reports `rpc_error{code="-32010"}`, and its page is inhibited by
  the `KardamomHaltDaLag` page.
- On staging after the deploy: one hour of the dashboard with a 100% success ratio, one
  deposit credited, one sampled transaction at `safe`, and a test failure (stop one ingress)
  that shows on the per-endpoint panels.

## 9. Decisions made in the build

These fill gaps of the sections above.

- **No `eth_call`.** The ingress serves no `eth_call` and no state read of a contract. The
  counter (`contracts/src/canary/CanaryCounter.sol`) takes exactly one wei per write and has
  no `receive`, so its balance equals its count. The `contract` probe reads
  `eth_getBalance` of the counter and compares it with the count in the write's
  `Incremented` event. A read under the count at the deadline is `timeout{stage="read"}`;
  any other value is `state_mismatch`. Only the ring's accounts can write the counter. The
  token and the pool emit what the probes check: `Supply` carries the total supply after each
  mint and burn; `Swap` and `Liquidity` carry the reserves before and after the call and a
  sequence number. The external read is `eth_getBalance` of the pool against its ETH reserve.
- **The submit.** The probes submit with `kardamom_sendRawTransactionAsync`, so "submit to
  hash" is the publish and the receipt has its own stage. `eth_sendRawTransaction` waits
  for the receipt, so its hash would carry the whole latency.
- **The nonce rule.** A JSON-RPC error on the first submit of a transaction is a refusal
  before the publish, so the nonce is free. Any other case keeps the transaction in the
  journal with a `published` mark, and a refusal of a resend does not free the nonce.
- **Outcomes.** `account_stalled`: every funded ring account is in use or blocked.
  `rpc_error{code="transport"}`: the call had no answer. Every `probe_total` series also has
  the `endpoint` label, for the per-endpoint panels.
- **Placement.** The job runs on the node whose role set holds `monitoring` (the aux node by
  default), beside the Prometheus that scrapes it. The ingress endpoints are the Consul node
  records `ingress-<i>.node.<dc>.consul:8545`, so a stopped ingress stays in the list and
  shows as failures of its own endpoint.
- **Keys.** The deploy writes the ring's mnemonic (`CANARY_MNEMONIC`) to the Nomad Variable
  `nomad/jobs/canary` as `KARDAMOM_CANARY_MNEMONIC`; `CANARY_RING_OFFSET` sets the first
  derivation index. Without a mnemonic, `CANARY_LOCAL=1` runs the canary of the local profile
  on the public anvil mnemonic from index 34; the dev genesis funds accounts #34 to #37.
  Otherwise no canary runs: the CI shards count the transactions they send, and the canary's
  transactions would add to those counts.
- **The chaos case of section 7.** The `chaos-l1` shard runs with the default DA-lag budget
  (10,000 blocks). A freeze past it does not fit the shard, and a small budget for the whole
  shard would refuse the load of the other `chaos-l1` cases. The case `canary-da-lag`
  (`crates/chaos/src/cases/l1_canary.rs`) runs by name on a cluster with a small budget, as
  `da-lag-halt` does, and with `CANARY_LOCAL=1`.
- **The market task.** One task owns the token and the pool and runs `rwa`, `swap` and
  `liquidity` on their timers, so no lock guards the reserve snapshot or the supply ledger.
  The ledger lives in `<dir>/market.json`, with the change in flight, so a restart between a
  mint and its receipt does not report a false `supply_mismatch`.
- **The pool.** The pool syncs its reserves to its balances at the start of each call and
  writes the reserves after the call before any transfer (checks, then effects, then
  interactions). Only allowlisted accounts hold the token, so only the ring can trade; ETH
  forced into the pool shows as a balance above the reserve, which the canary counts as
  outside activity.
- **Funding.** The first deposit (0.005 ETH by default) goes to the first ring account while it
  holds less than that; later deposits are 0.0001 ETH. The balance task tops each other ring
  account up with 0.0004 ETH from the first one when it falls under the floor.
- **The safe timeout.** The deploy passes twice the batcher's idle flush, and never under two
  minutes, since a post also waits for its L1 inclusion.
- **Fees.** The `fees` probe sends its own transfer with a tip of one wei per gas and a gas
  limit of 30,000, so the tip on unused gas shows. A base fee of zero means no schedule; then
  no tip is paid.
